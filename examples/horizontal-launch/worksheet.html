Horizontal Launch

Two motions, one clock

What a horizontal launch is

An object is launched horizontally when it leaves a surface moving sideways with no upward or downward velocity at the start. After that, gravity is the only thing acting on it. The path is a curve, but the physics is two simple motions that share one clock, the time t.

horizontal (x)vertical (y)
Starting velocitythe launch speed, vx,izero, vy,i
Accelerationzero, ax9.80 m/s2, directed downward
Kind of motionconstant velocityfree fall from rest

The motions are independent; only the time is shared. Find t in one direction, then use it in the other.

Equations

The kinematic equations apply separately to each direction. Replace x with y for vertical motion.

Δx=vx,it+12axt2
vf=vi+at
vf2=vi2+2aΔx

Near Earth’s surface, g=9.80 m/s2, directed downward. A velocity from its components, and its direction as an angle θ from the horizontal:

How to write a calculation

Choose a positive direction for each axis and write both at the top of your work. Every value you use gets its direction in words next to the sign.

  1. Equation: the general relationship, in symbols only.
  2. Solve for the unknown: rearrange, still in symbols.
  3. Plug-in: put each value in with its units, then give the result.

Example: a cart moves at 3.0 m/s to the right for 5.0 s. How far does it go?

Δx=vt
Δx=(3.0 m/s)(5.0 s)=15 m (right)

Part 1: a ball off a cliff

A ball rolls off the edge of a cliff 45.0 m above level ground. It leaves the edge moving horizontally at 12.0 m/s. It lands on the ground below.

Motion diagram: one dot every 0.606 s, from launch to just before landing.

Velocity-time graph for the whole flight.

(a) Find the time the ball is in the air.

Answer (a)

I chose right as positive for x and up as positive for y. The landing point is Δy=−45.0 m (downward) from the launch point, and ay=−9.80 m/s2 (downward).

equation
Δy=vy,it+12ayt2

The object leaves horizontally, so vy,i=0 and the first term is zero.

solve for the unknown
Δy=12ayt2⇒t=2Δyay

Plug in Δy=−45.0 m (downward) and ay=−9.80 m/s2 (downward).

plug-in
t=2(−45.0 m)(−9.80 m/s2)=3.03 s

Both Δy and ay are negative, so the ratio is positive. In t=2Δyay the launch speed vx,i never appears: it does not change the fall time.

(b) Find how far from the base of the cliff the ball lands.

Answer (b)

I chose right as positive for x and up as positive for y.

equation
Δx=vx,it+12axt2

Nothing pushes the ball sideways, so ax=0 and the equation reduces to Δx=vx,it. It already solves for Δx, so no rearrangement is needed.

plug-in
Δx=(12.0 m/s)(3.03 s)=+36.4 m (right)

(c) Find the ball’s velocity just before it lands: its speed, and its angle and direction in words. Show the vertical component first.

Answer (c)

I chose right as positive for x and up as positive for y. The vertical and horizontal motions are separate, so I find each landing component, then combine them.

equation
vy,f=vy,i+ayt

With vy,i=0 this already solves for vy,f.

Plug in ay=−9.80 m/s2 (downward).

plug-in
vy,f=(−9.80 m/s2)(3.03 s)=−29.7 m/s (downward)

The horizontal component is unchanged: vx,f=+12.0 m/s (right).

equation
v=vx2+vy2

Plug in vx=+12.0 m/s (right) and vy=−29.7 m/s (downward).

plug-in
v=(12.0 m/s)2+(−29.7 m/s)2=32.0 m/s

The angle is measured below the horizontal. The vertical leg is opposite that angle and the horizontal leg is adjacent to it.

equation
tan⁡θ=oppositeadjacent
solve for the unknown
θ=tan−1⁡(|vy||vx|)
plug-in
θ=tan−1⁡(29.7 m/s12.0 m/s)=68.0° (below the horizontal, toward the right)

(c) A second car is dropped from rest off the same platform at the same instant. It should reach the ground at the same time as the first car. Check: find its fall time.

Answer (c)

I chose right as positive for x and up as positive for y. The second car starts from rest, so vy,i=0. It falls Δy=−20.0 m (downward) with ay=−9.80 m/s2 (downward): the same numbers as in (a).

equation
Δy=vy,it+12ayt2

With vy,i=0 the first term is zero.

solve for the unknown
Δy=12ayt2⇒t=2Δyay

Plug in Δy=−20.0 m (downward) and ay=−9.80 m/s2 (downward).

plug-in
t=2(−20.0 m)(−9.80 m/s2)=2.02 s

The two fall times are equal. The time depends only on Δy and ay, and both cars have the same values of each, so sideways motion adds no time in the air.
